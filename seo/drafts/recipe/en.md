Everyone who cooks with us starts in the same place: a mound of flour and their own two hands. This is that dough, the egg pasta we teach at the table in our Oltrarno kitchen and live online, written down so you can make it at home without a machine.

## What is the original Italian fresh pasta dough?

Flour and eggs. In Tuscany, as across central and northern Italy, fresh egg pasta for ribbons and ravioli is made from soft wheat flour and fresh eggs. The ingredient list is short on purpose. The skill is in your hands, learning to tell by feel when the dough is ready, and that is exactly what we teach.

Eggs differ in size and flour absorbs more or less depending on the day, so treat any ratio as a starting point. The dough tells you the rest.

### Ingredients

- Flour: {{OWNER: flour type we use}}
- Fresh eggs, at {{OWNER: dough ratio, e.g. grams of flour per egg}}
- A little extra flour or semolina, for dusting

You also need a large wooden board (or a clean table), a fork, a bench scraper or a knife, a long rolling pin and a sharp knife.

### Method

1. Pour the flour onto a wooden board in a mound and make a wide well in the middle.
2. Crack the eggs into the well and beat them with a fork, drawing in flour from the inside wall of the well a little at a time.
3. When the centre turns into a thick paste, push in the rest of the flour with a bench scraper or your hands and press everything into one rough mass.
4. Knead for about ten minutes: push the dough away with the heel of your hand, fold it back, give it a quarter turn and repeat, until it is smooth and springs back slowly when you press it.
5. Wrap the dough and let it rest at room temperature for at least 30 minutes.
6. Roll it out on a lightly floured wooden board with a long rolling pin, working from the centre outwards and turning the sheet often, until it is thin enough to see the grain of the board through it.
7. Dust the sheet, roll it up loosely and slice it across into ribbons with a sharp knife, then shake the ribbons loose.
8. Cook in plenty of boiling salted water for a few minutes, until tender, and toss straight into the sauce.

## How do you knead pasta dough by hand?

With the heel of your hand, for about ten minutes, until the dough is smooth. Push it away, fold it back towards you, turn it a quarter and go again. Find a rhythm and keep it.

What to look for:

- **At the start** the dough is rough, patchy and a little dry in places. That is normal. Keep going.
- **When it is ready** it looks smooth and almost satiny, and a fingertip pressed into it leaves a dent that slowly fills back in.
- **If it sticks** to your hands and the board, dust it with a pinch of flour, no more.
- **If it cracks** and will not come together, wet your hands and keep kneading before you think about adding anything else.

Then let it rest. Kneading builds the gluten; resting relaxes it. Skip the rest and the dough fights the rolling pin and shrinks back every time you lift it.

## Can you make fresh pasta without a machine?

Yes, and for most of Italian history a rolling pin was the only way. A board and a long pin give you a sheet with a slightly rough surface that holds sauce beautifully.

- **Use wood.** A wooden board grips the dough a little as you roll. A stone or steel counter lets it slide.
- **Roll from the centre outwards,** never back and forth across the edges, and turn the sheet a quarter turn every few passes so it stays round and even.
- **Once the sheet is wider than your hands,** wrap the far edge around the pin and roll it towards you, stretching it gently as it winds.
- **Stop when you can see the grain of the board** through the dough. For filled pasta like [ravioli](/pasta-shapes/ravioli/) go a touch thinner still.
- **Work quickly.** The sheet dries as you roll, so keep the dough you are not using wrapped.

To cut ribbons by hand, let the sheet sit for a few minutes until it is no longer tacky, dust it, roll it up loosely like a newspaper and slice across: narrow for [tagliatelle](/pasta-shapes/tagliatelle/) and fettuccine, wide for [pappardelle](/pasta-shapes/pappardelle/). Our guests cut their fettuccine by hand in class too.

## Is there a Tuscan pasta dough without eggs?

Yes: the dough for [pici](/pasta-shapes/pici/). Tuscany's hand-rolled noodle has always been flour, water and olive oil, nothing else. You do not even need the rolling pin. You roll each piece between your palms and the board into a thick, slightly uneven strand, and the unevenness is the point.

It is also the dough we make for vegan guests at our [vegan and vegetarian class](/vegan-cooking-class-florence/). Learning to read a dough without egg is a skill of its own.

Not sure every sauce wants fresh pasta? It does not, and [fresh vs dry pasta in Italy](/blog/fresh-vs-dry-pasta-italy/) explains which goes with what.
