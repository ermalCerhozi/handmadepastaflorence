Florence rewards a slow evening and punishes the standard one: everyone at Piazzale Michelangelo in the same minute, then dinner at the first table with a view. We cook in the Oltrarno, on the quiet side of the river, and these are the things we tell couples to do. Ten ideas, roughly in the order we would do them over a long weekend.

**Romantic Florence in short:**

- **Best for**: couples, anniversaries, honeymoons and proposals.
- **Free**: the viewpoints, San Miniato al Monte, the Rose Garden and, in spring, the Iris Garden.
- **Seasonal**: wisteria at Giardino Bardini in April and May, the Iris Garden for about three weeks each spring, boat trips on the Arno from May to September.
- **Any month**: aperitivo in Piazza Santo Spirito, a pasta class for two, and a private table for a proposal.

## 1. Sunset at Piazzale Michelangelo, then up to San Miniato al Monte

Piazzale Michelangelo is the postcard: the whole city laid out below you, free, open every day, and full at sunset. Go anyway. Then keep climbing for five more minutes to **San Miniato al Monte**, the abbey church above it, where the crowd thins out.

Timing matters here. The abbey's own website says the basilica cannot be visited during services, and it lists vespers sung in Latin and Gregorian chant every afternoon at 18:30, a weekday Mass at 18:00 (also in chant), and on Sundays and holidays a Mass in chant at 17:30. So you choose: arrive before the service to see the interior, or stay and listen to the monks. Entry is free and needs no reservation. Check [sanminiatoalmonte.it](https://sanminiatoalmonte.it/contatti/) for the current times before you climb.

## 2. Golden hour on Ponte Santa Trinita

Most people crowd onto the Ponte Vecchio itself. Our pick is the next bridge downstream, **Ponte Santa Trinita**. Stand in the middle and look back east: in the evening the low sun is behind you, and it lights up the Ponte Vecchio instead of silhouetting it.

The bridge has a story that suits the hour. Cosimo I commissioned Bartolomeo Ammannati to rebuild it after the 1557 flood, the retreating German army destroyed it in 1944, and Florence rebuilt it in 1958 exactly as it was and where it was. It costs nothing and needs no booking.

## 3. The wisteria and the view at Giardino Bardini

The **Giardino Bardini** is four hectares of hillside between Costa San Giorgio and Borgo San Niccolò, with an Italian garden, an English wood and an orchard park, all looking out over the city. In April and May its wisteria pergola, about 70 metres long, turns into a tunnel of lilac, violet and pink. If you are in Florence then, this is the reason to go.

The garden ticket was €10 at full price when we checked, the garden is closed on the first and last Monday of every month, and last entry is one hour before closing. The café, which has a panoramic view, is open from March to November. Opening hours shift through the year and the garden's own pages do not all agree, so check [villabardini.it](https://www.villabardini.it/en/organize-your-visit/) on the day.

## 4. The Rose Garden and, in spring, the Iris Garden

The **Giardino delle Rose** is a green terrace just below Piazzale Michelangelo, and it is free. It makes a gentler stop than the piazza above it. The tourist board lists it as closing at 17:00 from November to February and staying open into the evening in summer, so in winter come before dark.

In spring, the **Giardino dell'Iris**, on Viale Michelangiolo, opens to the public for about three weeks during the iris bloom: 25 April to 20 May in 2026, free, with the dates moving from year to year. Outside that window, visits are by reservation only.

## 5. A boat on the Arno, May to September

The Renaioli association runs trips on the Arno in traditional *barchetti*, small flat-bottomed boats, by reservation from May to September. The ride takes about 45 minutes. It starts at Piazza Mentana, passes under the Ponte Vecchio and Ponte Santa Trinita, and ends at Ponte alla Carraia, so you see both bridges from below.

The tourist board page gives no prices or departure times, so ask the association directly at [renaioli.it](http://www.renaioli.it/) and tell them you would like a late-day departure.

## 6. A glass through a wine window

Look closely at the palace walls in the centre of Florence and you will find small arched doors, the *buchette del vino*, through which wine was once sold straight to the street. The Associazione Buchette del Vino counts 165 of them within the city and 28 outside it, and cites a 1634 plague chronicle that mentions them. Most are sealed or decorative now. A few have been restored and serve wine on request.

The association's census lists the window at **DiVin Boccone** on Via delle Caldaie, near Piazza Santo Spirito, as active again. Ask when you get there, because not every window pours and the hours are the venue's own. It is the cheapest romantic gesture on this list: a glass of wine handed to you through a wall, and a story to tell afterwards.

## 7. Aperitivo in Piazza Santo Spirito

Piazza Santo Spirito fills up at aperitivo hour with students, artisans and neighbours, which is exactly why it is the right place to end a sunset walk. Take a spritz on the piazza and let the evening go where it goes. When you are hungry, dinner is a short walk away in Santo Spirito or San Frediano, and our guide to [where to eat handmade pasta in Florence](/blog/where-to-eat-handmade-pasta-in-florence/) says where we would book. More of the neighbourhood is in our [Oltrarno guide](/blog/things-to-do-in-oltrarno-florence/).

## 8. A half-day in Fiesole

Fiesole is the hill town above Florence, and the best excuse to look back at the city from a distance. Bus 7 runs from the Santa Maria Novella station area to Piazza Mino in Fiesole, and the Fiesole tourist site says buses leave about every 20 minutes. Go in the morning, wander up to the Roman Theatre and the archaeological area on Largo F. Farulli, have lunch, and be back in the Oltrarno for the evening.

Opening hours and ticket prices for the theatre are on the [Musei di Fiesole website](https://www.museidifiesole.it/). Check them the day before, so you do not climb a hill to a closed gate.
