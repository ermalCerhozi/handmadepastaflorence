Florence has had student and cost-of-living marches since 16 October, as part of the protests across Italy. Each one was announced days ahead, with a time and a meeting point, and lasted a few hours. On a march day, keep away from the route for those hours and the rest of Florence carries on. A good way to spend that day is out of the city, in a farm kitchen in the hills.

## What is happening in Florence (updated 29 October 2026)

Since mid-October Italy has seen protests against the cost of living, fuel prices and the government's school policy. The worst of it was in Milan, Turin and Rome, where some marches ended in riots and looting. In Florence so far:

- **16 October.** More than 2,000 secondary school students marched from Piazza San Marco to Piazza del Carmine in the Oltrarno. In the days after, students occupied about a dozen Florence high schools.
- **24 October.** More than a thousand people marched in the afternoon from Piazza Santa Maria Novella to Piazza Santo Spirito, on a route agreed with the police. Part of the march then tried to reach the designer shops of Via Tornabuoni. Police held them on Ponte Santa Trinita for several hours, with eggs and firecrackers thrown and three baton charges, and the shops on Via Tornabuoni closed early. In the evening a smaller group went to Santa Maria Novella station and stopped the trams for over an hour. Local papers reported no injuries and no damage to shops that evening.
- **26 October.** About a thousand secondary school students marched from Santa Maria Novella against a visit by the education minister. When they tried to head for the Duomo, where he was, there was some pushing at a police line in the early afternoon. The march then ended in Piazza Indipendenza without further trouble.
- **30 October.** National general strike called by the USB union, with a march in Florence at 10:00 from the Fortezza da Basso. The government has ordered the transport part of the strike postponed (details below).
- **20 November.** General strike called by the CGIL union.

## Is Florence safe for tourists right now?

Florence has not had the riots seen in Milan and Turin. It has had marches that block a few streets for a few hours, and on 24 October a standoff on one bridge. Away from the route, the city carries on as normal.

Government advice says much the same. The [UK Foreign Office](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/italy) Italy page covers the riots in northern cities and the 30 October strike, and was last updated on 29 October. It does not advise against travel, and it does not mention Florence. The US keeps Italy at its usual Level 2, "exercise increased caution", and has issued demonstration alerts for Rome and Milan. Canada's advice for Italy is still "exercise a high degree of caution".

## Dates to plan around

**Thursday 29 October, 21:00, to Friday 30 October, 20:59.** The USB general strike was meant to stop trains for these 24 hours. On 27 October the transport minister ordered the strike postponed for trains, planes, buses and taxis, because transport strikes are not allowed in the holiday period around 1 November. Trenitalia and Italo say trains will run to the normal timetable. USB says it will strike anyway, so some trains may still be cancelled. Check your train that morning. The airport staff strike at Florence and Pisa on 30 October falls in the same holiday period, and no flight cancellations had been announced by 29 October. Check with your airline before you go.

**Friday 30 October, from 10:00.** USB march from Piazza Bambine e Bambini di Beslan, next to the Fortezza da Basso and a few minutes' walk from Santa Maria Novella station. The route had not been published on 29 October. Keep away from the Fortezza, the station and the centre that morning.

**Friday 20 November.** CGIL general strike, and a rail strike from 21:00 on 20 November to 20:00 on 21 November.

Italian strikes are often shortened or called off at the last minute. Check the day before on the transport ministry's [strike calendar](https://scioperi.mit.gov.it/) or with Trenitalia.

## How to plan a day around a march

Florence marches are announced two or three days ahead, and the time and meeting point are in the local papers (Firenze Today and La Nazione both cover them). On a march day:

- Book museum slots well before or well after the march time.
- Keep away from the meeting point and the route for the two or three hours of the march. So far that has meant Santa Maria Novella, Piazza San Marco, Piazza Indipendenza and the bridges toward the Oltrarno.
- Don't go to watch. Police can close the side streets around a march at short notice.
- Shops on the route may close early, as the designer shops on Via Tornabuoni did on 24 October. Leave the shopping for another day.
